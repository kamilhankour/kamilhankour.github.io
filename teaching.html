Teaching

My teaching is informed by the same questions that guide my research: how students make sense of ideas, how classroom environments shape participation, and how instructors can create opportunities for students to connect disciplinary content with what they already know and care about.

Teaching areas

  • Educational psychology
  • Learning and motivation
  • Student engagement
  • Classroom discourse and participation
  • Research methods
  • Quantitative, mixed, and computational approaches to educational research

Teaching approach

I aim to design courses in which students do more than learn about educational theories. Students should have repeated opportunities to use those theories to interpret authentic problems of teaching and learning, evaluate evidence, and develop explanations of their own.

Three principles guide my teaching:

Make theory usable

Educational psychology is most powerful when students can use theoretical ideas as analytic tools. I emphasize application to classroom cases, research findings, instructional decisions, and students' own experiences as learners.

Treat participation as part of learning

Discussion, explanation, questioning, and collaborative problem solving are not additions to learning. They are among the processes through which learning occurs. I therefore design opportunities for students to articulate, test, revise, and connect ideas with others.

Make methods transparent

Whether students are reading a study or conducting research themselves, I emphasize the connection among research questions, constructs, evidence, analysis, and claims. Students should be able to understand not only what a study found, but how researchers came to know it and what the evidence can reasonably support.
