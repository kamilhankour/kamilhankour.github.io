Research agenda

My research asks a broad question: How can learning environments expand students' opportunities to participate meaningfully in science? I approach this question across three connected areas.

Discourse & participation

I study how teacher-student and peer discourse shape students' opportunities to exercise agency, collaborate, and participate in active sensemaking.

Engagement & relevance

I examine how students experience engagement in real classrooms, including how percieved relevance, funds of knowledge, self-efficacy, belonging, and other contextual and person-level factors relate to engagement.

Methodology

I develop and evaluate ways to use natural language processing, machine learning, and multimodal classroom data to study discourse at a scale that remains grounded in educational theory and qualitative interpretation.

Current work

IES-funded research-practice partnership

Hybrid²: Creating Equitable Spaces for Science Discourse in Blended Learning Environments

I serve as project manager for the Missouri side of Hybrid², an IES-funded research practice partnership led by Dr. Kui Xie at the University of Missouri and Drs. Christine Lee Bae & John Fife at Virginia Commonwealth University. The project examines how locally and culturally relevant phenomena, combined with digital technologies, can support engaging and discourse-rich learning in middle school science classrooms.
